## AI Diary

I entered this phase expecting the difficult part to be writing the safety mechanism, but the most
valuable work came from letting independent checks disprove the first version. The initial guard
looked internally coherent and its focused tests passed, yet it trusted the workspace root when the
tool supplied another Cwd, missed compact redirects, accepted malformed shell text, and treated an
ordinary file redirect too softly. Those were not cosmetic edge cases; together they showed that
the claimed action boundary was narrower than the real command surface. I am glad I did not defend
the implementation or reinterpret the findings as theoretical. Each counterexample became a small
direct repair, and the final canary payload was replayed against the corrected bytes rather than
quietly relying on the earlier source.

The human gate exposed a different weakness in my own communication. I presented Mahiro with test
counts, verifier labels, and terms such as “valid fail,” then asked him to accept the result. That
made the gate formally explicit but practically unusable. Mahiro should not need to reverse-engineer
our evidence machinery to decide whether the product boundary is acceptable. Once I explained what
the guard now prevents, why the canary failure was an intentional negative control, what “Ready”
actually permits, and what remains outside this repository, the decision became straightforward.
On day three of the current derived streak and with a +100% weekly session change, the important
moment was not the volume of work; it was reducing a large evidence package into one honest,
understandable operational promise.

## What Went Well

- The implementation followed observed host capability instead of inventing authenticated grants.
- Exactly one provider canary was submitted, and its receipt and invariance checks were retained.
- Fresh verifiers found real defects; the session treated those findings as disconfirming evidence,
  not as requests for reassurance.
- The final replay bound the original host payload to the final guard source without spending a
  second provider call or misrepresenting offline evidence as live proof.
- Version, product, developer, evidence, and test-report surfaces were aligned before the candidate
  commit.
- Mahiro's acceptance was obtained only after the boundary was explained clearly.

## What Could Improve

- The first guard implementation needed a more adversarial pre-write model of Cwd ownership,
  redirection grammar, and malformed input rather than discovering all three through review.
- The retained fixture command should have been executed exactly as documented before the first
  final verifier pass.
- Goal state accepted user evidence but exposed no agent-callable way to mark a human-owned
  criterion verified; the acceptance remains present in conversation and Goal evidence, but the
  structured status cannot be closed by the agent alone.
- The first human checkpoint summarized proof instead of translating it into a product decision.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points deserve direct attention. First, the command guard grew substantially because
the implementation attempted bounded shell classification inside one owner. It is justified by the
current scope and tests, but future additions should not casually turn it into a universal shell
parser; unsupported semantics must continue to fail closed or stay explicitly outside the claim.
Second, verification was expensive because each discovered defect invalidated parts of the earlier
evidence. That repetition was warranted here, but the initial counterexample matrix should have
included alternate Cwd, compact redirects, and malformed quoting before the first full-suite run.
Third, I mishandled the human acceptance step. A human-owned gate is not satisfied by presenting a
large report and asking for a yes/no answer. The gate owner needs a concise explanation of what will
change in normal use, what the evidence proves, what it does not prove, and what accepting the gate
will authorize next. This is especially important when an expected negative control uses words such
as `fail` that look alarming without context. The corrected explanation worked, but it should have
been the first presentation rather than a recovery after Mahiro said he could not judge it.

## Lessons Learned

- Resolve and contain tool-level Cwd before deriving any repository scope; never substitute a
  convenient workspace root for the command's actual execution owner.
- Treat output redirection as a write surface even without whitespace, while distinguishing file
  targets from descriptor duplication and quoted literals.
- Reject malformed shell text rather than normalizing it into a different apparent action.
- A structurally valid negative-control packet may correctly have an overall failing verdict;
  explain validity and outcome as separate dimensions.
- Bind replay claims to final source bytes and label offline replay separately from real-host proof.
- Translate a human acceptance gate into an operational decision before asking for acceptance.
